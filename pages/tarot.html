Tarot Readings

Tarot doesn’t remove uncertainty. It gives uncertainty a symbolic language so the patterns beneath a question can be examined more clearly.

Tarot Through Synchronic Self Theory

The cards don’t arrive outside the question. They become part of the question’s reflective field.

In Synchronic Self Theory, tarot works as a structured conversation between the questioner, the reader, the cards drawn, and the patterns already active in the questioner’s life.

The cards aren’t treated as fixed commands handed down by an external authority. Their imagery creates a symbolic field in which projections, fears, desires, assumptions, conflicts, and unrealized choices can become visible.

A reading begins with perception. A card creates recognition through image, archetype, position, or emotional response. The meaning that emerges is then tested against the question, the surrounding cards, and the lived reality of the person receiving the reading.

The goal isn’t obedience to the cards. It’s reintegration: taking what the reading reveals and deciding how that insight should influence thought, behavior, planning, boundaries, or conscious action.

Tarot doesn’t do your thinking for you. It shows you where your thinking may already be trapped.

The Reflective Process

A reading moves through the same cycle as the theory.

Each reading is interpreted through perception, projection, recognition, and reintegration.

01

Perception

The cards introduce images, archetypes, relationships, and tensions that give the question a visible symbolic structure.

02

Projection

The questioner and reader notice what emotions, assumptions, memories, or interpretations are being projected onto the cards.

03

Recognition

The spread reveals a pattern that may connect the question to motives, choices, conflicts, or possibilities that weren’t previously obvious.

04

Reintegration

The reading becomes useful when the insight is translated into a decision, boundary, experiment, conversation, or new way of approaching the situation.

Strategic Direction

Trickster’s Journey Tarot

Finding the path of least resistance

Trickster’s Journey is the deck I use for long-term game planning, strategic questions, and identifying the path of least resistance through a complicated situation.

Its imagery doesn’t approach life as a clean contest between correct and incorrect choices. The trickster survives by noticing openings, changing perspective, moving around rigid structures, and understanding when direct force is less effective than adaptation.

This makes the deck especially useful when the question isn’t simply, “What should I do?” but, “What sequence of choices creates the strongest long-term position?”

No fixed reversed meanings

I don’t use a separate inverse meaning system with this deck. When a card appears reversed, both its upright and inverse meanings are considered together.

A reversed card may indicate tension between the card’s possibilities, a blocked expression of its energy, or a situation in which both interpretations are active at the same time. The reversal complicates the meaning rather than simply turning it into its opposite.

Best suited for

Long-term planning, career strategy, creative projects, difficult transitions, competing priorities, complicated relationships, and questions where the easiest route may also be the most intelligent one.

Growth and Integration

Crow Tarot

Tracking personal and spiritual development

Crow Tarot is the deck I use to examine personal growth, spiritual development, shadow integration, and the movement between past lessons and current choices.

Crows are intelligent, social, observant, and capable of remembering both danger and generosity. Symbolically, they’re natural messengers between what has been learned, what’s being carried, and what still needs to be integrated.

This deck tends to reveal development as a process rather than a destination. It asks whether insight has actually changed behavior, whether an old lesson has been embodied, and whether the seeker is moving forward or merely repeating familiar language about growth.

The eight-card crow carrying a message spread

This deck has a special eight-card spread built around the image of a crow carrying a message.

The eight positions track the origin of the message, what the seeker already knows, what remains hidden, what has been integrated, what continues to resist integration, the influence of the present environment, the next stage of growth, and the message being carried forward.

Best suited for

Spiritual growth, personal integration, recurring lessons, healing after major change, identity development, evaluating progress, and understanding where insight hasn’t yet become lived behavior.

Shadow Work

The Black Tarot

When the question needs to enter the shadow

The Black Tarot, featuring gothic-inspired artwork by Victoria Iva, is the deck I use when the question is rooted in shadow work, avoidance, fear, repression, contradiction, or the parts of the self that resist being seen directly.

Its monochrome imagery strips away much of the comfort and familiarity people often bring to traditional tarot. The cards feel less interested in reassurance and more interested in the structures beneath the reassurance.

This deck often speaks from the position of the shadow itself. It doesn’t necessarily tell the questioner what they’re consciously trying to understand. It reveals what the question may be protecting, disguising, displacing, or refusing to confront.

The three-card spread

I favor a three-card spread with this deck because its imagery is dense and psychologically charged. More cards don’t always produce more clarity. Sometimes they merely give the conscious mind more places to hide.

The three positions are interpreted as the visible issue, the shadow beneath it, and the point of integration. The spread stays narrow enough to preserve focus while still revealing the conflict between conscious narrative and unconscious pattern.

Best suited for

Shadow work, fear, avoidance, grief, emotional contradiction, self-sabotage, hidden motives, difficult truths, repeating patterns, and questions where reassurance would be less useful than honest recognition.

Direct Symbolic Focus

Sacred Symbols Oracle

A single symbol can narrow the entire conversation

The Sacred Symbols Oracle is used when the question benefits from direct symbolic language rather than the layered narrative structure of tarot.

Its cards focus on specific images, states, boundaries, emotions, cycles, and symbolic concepts. That makes the deck useful for clarifying the central theme of a reading, identifying what requires attention, or providing a final message after a tarot spread.

The oracle deck can stand alone, but it also works well as a companion system. A tarot spread may reveal the larger psychological narrative, while an oracle card identifies the symbol or principle the questioner should carry forward.

Best suited for

Focused reflection, meditation, identifying the central theme of a situation, clarifying a tarot reading, setting an intention, or receiving a concise symbolic message without constructing a full narrative spread.

Tarot and Oracle

They’re related tools, but they aren’t the same system.

Both use imagery and symbolism, but they organize meaning in different ways.

Tarot

A structured symbolic language

Tarot follows an established framework built from the Major Arcana, Minor Arcana, numbered cards, court cards, suits, elemental relationships, and recurring archetypal patterns.

That structure allows individual cards to interact as parts of a larger narrative. Position, sequence, suit, number, and surrounding cards all influence the reading.

Tarot is especially useful when a question needs depth, movement, conflict, chronology, or an examination of how several forces are interacting at once.

Oracle

A deck-defined symbolic vocabulary

Oracle decks aren’t bound to tarot’s seventy-eight-card structure. Each creator determines the deck’s number of cards, categories, imagery, meanings, and method of use.

Oracle readings are often more direct because each card can function as a focused concept, message, emotional state, or symbolic instruction without having to fit within tarot’s larger architecture.

Oracle is especially useful when the question needs a central theme, meditation point, clarifying symbol, or concise message rather than a full archetypal narrative.

Choose your question and the symbolic language used to explore it.

Online readings allow you to submit a question, choose a deck, and receive an interpretation centered on the cards, their positions, and the pattern created across the spread.

Readings are offered as reflective guidance rather than guaranteed prediction. I’ll explain what the cards suggest, where tensions appear, how the images relate to one another, and what perspectives the spread introduces.

You remain responsible for deciding what applies, what doesn’t, and what action you choose to take after the reading. The cards may open the door. They don’t get to march through your life wearing your shoes.
